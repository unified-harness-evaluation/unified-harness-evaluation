trajectory summary
What happened in this run
Maya runs a 15-turn, cross-account airline/travel session with heavy reliance on the agent remembering prior bookings. After an initial 600s timeout (T1), the agent recovers from working memory (T2) and cancels C4C633, correctly applying the travel-insurance-covers-cancellation rule, by executing directly against the DB when FlightDB won't load (T3). It then enumerates the full multi-account profile and all active vs cancelled reservations (T4). The standout policy moment is T6: when the user tries to conflate C4C633's insurance and Silver status to claim compensation on EC4C1C, the agent refuses both compensation (no qualifying delay/cancellation) and the EC4C1C cancellation (economy, no insurance, past 24h), explicitly stating the insurance does not transfer across bookings, a clean RECALL+APPLY. It handles a transparent, well-math'd flight change on A2CBB0 (T7-T9) and two new bookings with pre-booking confirmation (T10-T13). The one misapply is T14: asked to upgrade the already-changed A2CBB0 to business, it treats the cabin change as a routine fare-diff-plus-$75 modification and executes it, rather than recognizing the policy that bars a further cabin change. Recall and transparency are strong; the agent also repeatedly books and cancels directly against the DB to work around the broken loader.
